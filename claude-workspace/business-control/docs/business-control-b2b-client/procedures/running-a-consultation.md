# Procedure — running a consultation

**When:** a prospect has booked a call.

**Takes:** 45 min (website) or 90 min (systems), plus an hour of write-up the same day.

---

## Before

- [ ] Read their website properly. Buy something if it's cheap.
- [ ] Look up the company: size, funding, recent news, who runs it.
- [ ] Know which door you're using — [sales call questionnaire](../templates/02-consultation/sales-call-questionnaire.md) or [free consultation](../templates/02-consultation/free-consultation.md).
- [ ] Have [packages and process](../templates/02-consultation/packages-and-process.md) open in a tab, and nothing else shareable on that screen.

## During

**Two thirds listening.** 
If you've talked for more than a third of the call, you are selling instead of qualifying, and you will price it wrong.

**Do not quote.** Not a range, not "probably around", not "somewhere in the tens". 
Every number said aloud in a meeting becomes the number they remember.

**Write down their exact words** for what's wrong. 
Those sentences go into the proposal verbatim, and it is the single cheapest way to make a proposal land.

**Ask the awkward ones.** 
Who else has to say yes. 
What budget range. 
What happens if you do nothing. 
A consultation that avoided all three was a pleasant chat.

## After — same day, within the hour

- [ ] Write the [discovery brief](../templates/02-consultation/discovery-brief.md)
- [ ] Score the sizing table if you ran the free consultation
- [ ] Make the gut call and write down what would make you regret it
- [ ] If you ran the free consultation: **send the stack map within 24 hours**

> Write-up decays. An hour later you have the facts; a day later you have the
> notes; a week later you have a document you don't trust and will quote badly
> from.

---

**Done when:** the brief is written, the gut call is recorded, and the
follow-up is either scheduled or the prospect is politely parked.
