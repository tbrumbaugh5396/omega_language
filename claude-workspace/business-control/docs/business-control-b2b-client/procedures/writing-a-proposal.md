# Procedure — writing a proposal

**When:** within 3 working days of the consultation. 
Later than that and the urgency you found on the call has evaporated.

**Takes:** 2–3 hours.

---

## 1. Start from what they said

Section 1 of the proposal is their words, not yours. 
Pull the direct quote out of the consultation notes. 
If they read nothing else, they should read that paragraph and think *yes, they get it*.

## 2. Price from the rate card, never from the hours

Work out the hours internally to check the rate; quote the tier. 
Hourly punishes you for getting faster and invites arguments about how long something "should" take.

## 3. Build the three options

- **A — the floor.** Solves the stated problem, nothing more.
- **B — the recommended.** What you'd actually do, with the one or two add-ons
  that most affect the outcome. Say plainly that it's what you'd choose.
- **C — the everything.** Prices B by comparison. Some people take it.

Rough spacing: if B is $12k, A is around $7k and C around $22k.

## 4. Check the implied rate

Price ÷ estimated hours. 
**If it's under your floor, stop.** 
The scope is wrong, and finding that out now costs an hour; finding it out in week six costs the project.

## 5. Put dates on everything

Firm start, firm launch, **valid until [date + 14 days]**. 
An open-ended price is an invitation to come back in six months expecting the same number.

## 6. Send it, then follow up

Send by email with a two-line summary in the body — some people never open the attachment. 
Follow up on day 3 and day 10. 
Then stop; a third chase reads as need, and need costs you the price.

---

**Done when:** sent, dated, logged in the status board, and the follow-ups are in your calendar.
