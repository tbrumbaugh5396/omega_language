# Stage 02 — Understanding what they need
Two doors, and you pick one. Both end at the same place: a written internal
brief you can price from.

| Document | Goes to | When |
|---|---|---|
| `sales-call-questionnaire.md` | Internal | "We need a website" — 45 min on the business |
| `free-consultation.md` | Internal | "Everything behind the shop is spreadsheets" — 90 min on the machinery |
| `packages-and-process.md` | **Client** | Shared on screen at the end, sent after |
| `capability-menu.md` | **Client** | Sent with it — the software priced line by line |
| `discovery-brief.md` | Internal — never send | Written the same day, while it's fresh |

**Never quote on the call.** A number invented in a meeting is a number one of
you regrets. Say you'll come back within three days, and do.

**The menu is sent, not presented.** `packages-and-process.md` prices the work
to build it, once; `capability-menu.md` prices the software they run on,
monthly. Both go out after the call, so they read them without you in the room
— reading a price list aloud turns scoping into haggling.

**What you owe them:** if you ran the free consultation, the written map of
their stack within 24 hours — you promised it, it's the reason the
consultation was worth their time, and it costs you an hour.

**Gate:** the discovery brief is written, including the honest gut call. If you
can't write the one-liner, you don't understand the project well enough to
price it.
