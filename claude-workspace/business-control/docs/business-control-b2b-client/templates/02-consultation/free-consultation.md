# Free consultation — [CLIENT]

**60–75 minutes. No charge. No quote on the call.**

The sales call questionnaire asks about the *business*. This asks about the
*machinery* — what they run today, what it costs, where it leaks, and which
parts of the platform are actually worth building for them.

Run this when the conversation is about more than a website: a business that
already has systems, staff who need different views of them, or someone who
says "we've got a shop but everything behind it is spreadsheets".

**Client:** ______________ **Date:** ______ **Attendees:** ______________
**Their systems person, if any:** ______________

> **Free is a deliberate choice, not a giveaway.** You leave with the
> assessment, they leave with a written map of their own stack that is worth
> having even if they never hire you. That map is why the quote lands as an
> obvious next step rather than a cold number. It also costs you 90 minutes,
> which is cheap insurance against quoting a project you didn't understand.

---

## How the session runs — say this at the top

> "Ninety minutes, no charge, no obligation. I want to map what you're running
> today and where the manual work is. At the end you'll get that map in
> writing whether or not we work together. Then I'll go away and come back
> within three days with a proposal — three options, firm numbers, firm dates.
> I won't quote on the call, because a number invented in a meeting is a
> number one of us regrets."

Two thirds listening. Share your screen only for [the process and packages
leave-behind](packages-and-process.md), and only at the end.

**Total known software spend:** $______/mo → **$______/yr**

> Add it up out loud, in front of them. Most owners have never seen the total.

### Constraints to surface now, not in week six

- [ ] Contracts or renewals that lock a system in until: ______________
- [ ] An incumbent agency or IT provider: ______________
- [ ] Regulatory / compliance obligations: ______________
- [ ] Someone internal whose job is tied to a system you'd replace: ______
- [ ] Hardware in play — scanners, printers, POS, tablets: ______________
- [ ] Where staff actually work: ☐ desk ☐ phone ☐ warehouse ☐ vehicle ☐ store

> That fourth one is not cynicism. If a system goes, someone's role changes,
> and if that person is in the room in week four with no warning, the project
> stalls. Find them now and give them something better to do.

### Sizing → tier

Score, then sanity-check against your gut. The score is a starting point, not
a verdict.

| Factor | Points |
|---|---|
| Each **must** module | 2 |
| Each **want** module deferred to a later phase | 0 |
| Each role needing a distinct view beyond owner + customer | 2 |
| Each listed integration | 3 |
| Each new connector not on the list | 8 |
| Each migration with a clean export | 1 |
| Each migration with no export | 5 |
| Custom pricing rules — wholesale tiers, contracts | 4 |
| Compliance obligation with evidence requirements | 5 |
| Hardware in the loop — scanners, printers, POS | 4 |

**Total:** ______

| Score | Package | Typical range | Typical timeline |
|---|---|---|---|
| Under 12 | **Storefront** | $[X,XXX] | [N] weeks |
| 12 – 30 | **Storefront + one operating area** | $[XX,XXX] | [N] weeks |
| 30 – 55 | **Whole business** | $[XX,XXX] | [N] weeks |
| Over 55 | **Whole business, phased** — quote phase 1 firm, later phases indicative | $[XX,XXX]+ | [N] weeks per phase |

> Over 55, quoting the whole thing as one number is how studios lose money and
> clients lose faith. Quote phase 1 firmly and everything after it as a range,
> and say plainly why: nobody can price month nine accurately in month zero,
> and pretending otherwise serves neither of you.

**Sanity check:** implied rate at this price is $______/hr. If it's under your
floor, the scope is wrong — fix it before the proposal, not in week six.
