# Stage 07 — Brand exploration

**Optional. Run it when the look is the point.**

| Tier | Run this stage? |
|---|---|
| Week website | No — they have a brand, you're applying it |
| Custom build | Only if the brand is thin or inconsistent |
| Custom build + Branding & creative | Always — the identity is the point |
| Branding & creative | Always — this *is* the project |

| Document | Goes to | When |
|---|---|---|
| `brand-exploration-brief.md` | Internal — never send | Written before you open a design tool |
| `direction-review-form.md` | **Client** | With the directions, for one consolidated response |
| `art-direction.md` | **Client** | The deliverable — filled in after they choose, then signed |

Inputs come from the [branding
questionnaire](../05-kickoff/branding-questionnaire.md) at kickoff and the
signed [requirements](../06-requirements/requirements-template.md). Don't start
without both: exploring a look before you know what the thing does produces
beautiful work that has to be redone.

---

## Why this is its own stage

Because "I'll know it when I see it" is not a brief, and answering it inside
the build stage is how a fortnight disappears. Separating it does three things:

**It makes taste a decision with a date on it.** A signed art direction is the
thing you point at when a stakeholder appears in week nine with opinions about
the blue.

**It bounds the exploration.** Two or three directions, one round, one choice.
Left unbounded, exploration expands to fill whatever time exists, and it is the
easiest work in the studio to keep fiddling with.

**It prices honestly.** Exploration is real work with real hours. Charged as a
stage, it's a line item; buried in "design", it's free.

---

## How it runs

**Two or three directions. Never one, never five.** One is a decision you made
for them and they will fight it to prove they were consulted. Five is a
committee, and the discussion becomes an average of everything rather than a
choice.

**Each direction is a genuine position**, not a colour variant of the same
idea. Give each a name and one sentence of argument — "Quiet Authority", "the
brand as a calm expert who doesn't shout". Clients choose between arguments far
better than between pictures.

**Show them in context.** A palette on a white board means nothing. Show the
homepage, the phone, the can, the email — whatever they'll actually look at.

**One round.** Consolidated feedback within five working days, then a choice.
Further exploration is a paid add-on, said warmly and every time.

> **The one that gets picked is rarely the one you'd frame.** Your job is the
> one that fits the business, and the surest way to lose the argument is to
> present a favourite and two strawmen — clients can tell, and it costs you the
> trust you need for every judgement call afterwards.

---

**Gate:** signed [art direction](art-direction.md). Design and build do not
start before it. Anything that contradicts it afterwards is a change order —
including "can we just try it in a different colour".
