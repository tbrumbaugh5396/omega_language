# Email scripts

Short, warm, specific. Fill the `[BRACKETS]`, delete the rest. Never send a
wall of text to someone who wrote you three lines.

---

## 1. First reply to an enquiry

> **Subject:** Re: [THEIR SUBJECT]
>
> Hi [NAME],
>
> Thanks for getting in touch — [ONE SPECIFIC THING FROM THEIR MESSAGE, so
> it's obvious you read it].
>
> The most useful next step is a short call, 30–45 minutes, so I can
> understand the business before I suggest anything. No pitch — mostly me
> asking questions.
>
> Here's my calendar: [LINK]
>
> If it helps beforehand, a couple of things I'll ask: what's not working
> about the current site, and what would have to be true in a year for this to
> have been worth doing.
>
> [YOUR NAME]

## 2. Politely declining

> Hi [NAME],
>
> Thanks for thinking of me. Being straight with you: this isn't a good fit —
> [REASON: the budget is below where I can do this properly / the timeline
> isn't one I can hit without cutting corners / this needs a specialist in X].
>
> Two people I'd genuinely recommend: [NAME, LINK] and [NAME, LINK].
>
> Good luck with it — I'd like to hear how it goes.
>
> [YOUR NAME]

## 3. After the call, before the proposal

> Hi [NAME],
>
> Good to talk. So we're on the same page, here's what I heard:
>
> - **The problem:** [ONE LINE]
> - **What success looks like:** [ONE LINE]
> - **Roughly:** [SCOPE], live by [DATE]
>
> Anything I've got wrong, tell me now — it's cheaper than fixing it later.
>
> Proposal with you by [DAY]. It'll have three options so you can see the
> trade-offs.
>
> [YOUR NAME]

## 4. Sending the proposal

> **Subject:** Proposal — [PROJECT]
>
> Hi [NAME],
>
> Attached. Three options; the middle one is what I'd choose in your position,
> and page [N] says why.
>
> Worth knowing:
>
> - **Timeline:** [X] weeks from deposit, assuming content arrives on schedule
> - **Included:** two rounds of feedback — one on the homepage direction, one
>   on the full site
> - **To start:** signed agreement plus the [X]% deposit
>
> Valid for 14 days. Happy to walk through it live if that's easier — reply
> and I'll send a time.
>
> [YOUR NAME]

## 5. Proposal follow-up (day 5, then day 12)

**Day 5:**

> Hi [NAME] — just checking this landed, and whether anything needs
> explaining. No rush, happy to answer questions.

**Day 12 (the honest close):**

> Hi [NAME],
>
> The proposal expires [DATE] and I'm scheduling [MONTH] now. If you'd like
> the slot I'll hold it for a few more days — if the timing's wrong, tell me
> and I'll come back in [N] months.
>
> Either answer is fine; I'd just rather know than guess.

## 6. Signed — kickoff

> **Subject:** We're on — here's what happens next
>
> Hi [NAME], deposit received. Thank you.
>
> Attached is the welcome guide — how we work, what I need from you and when.
> The short version:
>
> 1. **This week:** I'll send the requirements doc for you to approve
> 2. **By [DATE]:** I need your content and images (checklists attached)
> 3. **[DATE]:** homepage design for round 1 of feedback
> 4. **[DATE]:** full site for round 2
> 5. **[DATE]:** launch
>
> The thing that decides whether we hit that date is content. If it's going to
> be slow, tell me early and we'll plan around it.
>
> [YOUR NAME]

## 7. Chasing content (escalating, all friendly)

**Nudge — week 1:**

> Hi [NAME], how's the content coming? Anything I can do to unblock it — even
> rough notes I can shape are better than waiting for perfect.

**Nudge — week 2, with the consequence stated:**

> Hi [NAME],
>
> Still missing [SPECIFIC ITEMS]. I've built everything I can around the gaps.
>
> To be straight about it: the [DATE] launch assumed content by [DATE]. Every
> week it slips, that moves too — not a penalty, just how the schedule works.
>
> Would it help if I wrote a first draft for you to correct? That's [$X] and
> it's usually faster than writing from scratch.

**Formal pause — week 4:**

> Hi [NAME],
>
> Since I don't have [ITEMS], I'm pausing the project as of today and moving
> to my next booking. Nothing lost — everything built so far is safe.
>
> When you're ready, send the content and I'll schedule the restart for the
> next available slot, usually 2–3 weeks out. Per our agreement, a restart
> after 30 days carries a [$X] rescheduling fee.
>
> Not trying to be difficult — I'd rather tell you plainly than let it drift.

## 8. Delivering round 1 (homepage)

> **Subject:** Homepage — ready for you
>
> Hi [NAME], here it is: [LINK]
>
> This is **round 1 of 2**, and it's about direction, not detail. Does the
> feel match the brand? Is the structure right? Is the message landing?
>
> Don't worry about typos or exact images yet — that's round 2.
>
> Feedback form: [LINK]. One consolidated response by [DATE +5 DAYS] please —
> collecting it in one go is what keeps this on schedule.

## 9. Delivering round 2 (full site)

> **Subject:** Full site — round 2
>
> Hi [NAME], everything's here: [LINK]
>
> This is **round 2 of 2**, the polish pass — typos, images, links, anything
> that reads wrong on a phone.
>
> Form: [LINK], by [DATE +5 DAYS].
>
> After this round I'll make the changes and we're into launch prep. If
> something comes up that's genuinely new rather than a fix, that's fine —
> I'll quote it separately so it doesn't hold up the launch.

## 10. Scope creep, answered warmly

> Hi [NAME],
>
> Good idea, and easy to do — it's just outside what we scoped, so it needs to
> be its own small piece of work rather than absorbed into this one.
>
> [WHAT IT IS]: [$X], adds [N] days.
>
> Two options: add it now, or note it for after launch — often better, because
> you'll have real traffic telling you whether you need it.
>
> Want me to send the change order?

## 11. Third feedback round requested

> Hi [NAME],
>
> Happy to do another pass. We've used both rounds included in the project, so
> a third is [$X] — that covers the changes below plus a final check.
>
> [LIST WHAT THEY ASKED FOR]
>
> Say the word and I'll get on it. Or if you'd rather launch and refine with
> real visitors, that works too — plenty of clients find their list changes
> once the site is live.

## 12. Launch day

> **Subject:** [SITE] is live
>
> Hi [NAME], it's live: [URL]
>
> Handover doc attached — logins, how to edit things, where everything lives.
>
> I'll keep an eye on it for the next [N] days; if anything looks off, mail me
> and I'll fix it.
>
> Congratulations. It's a big improvement on where we started.

## 13. Invoice reminder

**Day 3 after due:**

**Day 10:**

> Hi [NAME],
>
> Invoice [#] is now [N] days overdue. Per the agreement, late payments carry
> [X]% after 14 days, and I'd rather not add that.
>
> If there's a hold-up on your side, tell me and we'll sort a plan. If it's
> just been missed: [LINK]

## 14. Care plan pitch (send at handover)

> Hi [NAME],
>
> Now the site's live, worth thinking about who keeps it that way — updates,
> backups, security patches, the small edits that pile up.
>
> I offer a care plan at [$X]/month: [WHAT'S IN IT], plus [N] hours of changes
> and priority on anything urgent.
>
> No pressure. If you'd rather call me when you need something, my hourly is
> [$X] and I'll always try to help — the plan just means you're never at the
> back of the queue.

## 15. Testimonial request (2 weeks after launch)

> Hi [NAME],
>
> Hope the new site's settling in well — [SPECIFIC THING YOU'VE NOTICED].
>
> Would you write me a couple of sentences I can use? Honestly the most useful
> ones answer: what was the problem, what was working with me like, and what's
> different now.
>
> Three lines is plenty. And if you'd be up for a short case study — a few
> numbers, a couple of screenshots — I'd love that too, and you'd get a link
> back from a site that ranks.
