## Writing guidance

**Headlines.** Say the thing. "Sparkling tea that takes the edge off" beats
"Welcome to our website". If it could be on a competitor's site, rewrite it.

**Length.** Shorter than you think. People scan. If a paragraph is over four
lines it's two paragraphs.

**Second person.** "You" not "our clients". "We'll help you" not "the company
provides assistance to".

**Specifics beat adjectives.** "Delivered in 3 days" beats "fast delivery".
"Used by 400 cafés" beats "trusted by many". Numbers are the cheapest
credibility available.

**Every page needs one job.** If a page is trying to do three things, it needs
to be three pages or one decision.

**Write the call to action first.** If you know what you want the visitor to
do, the rest of the page is easier to write.

## If content is stuck

Honest options, in order of how often they work:

1. **Send bullet points.** I'll turn them into copy. Often the fastest path.
2. **Talk it through.** 30 minutes recorded, I write from the transcript.
   People say better things out loud than they write.
3. **Copywriting add-on.** [$X]/page or [$X] for the site. Includes a round of
   your corrections.
4. **Launch with fewer pages.** A five-page site that exists beats a
   twelve-page site that doesn't. Add the rest later.

What doesn't work is waiting. If a page has been "nearly done" for two weeks,
it isn't — pick one of the above.
