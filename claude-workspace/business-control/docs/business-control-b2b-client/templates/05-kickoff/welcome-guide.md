## How feedback works

Your project includes **two rounds of feedback**. They do different jobs, and
knowing which is which saves everyone a wasted pass.

### Round 1 — the homepage
About **direction**. Does it feel like your brand? Is the structure right? Is
the message landing?

Don't worry about typos, final photos or exact wording — those change in round
2 anyway, and reviewing them twice is work neither of us needs.

### Round 2 — the full site
About **polish**. Copy, images, links, spacing, how it reads on a phone.

### Both rounds
- I'll send a **form**. Please use it — structured feedback is faster to act
  on than a thread, and nothing gets lost.
- **One consolidated response within five working days.**
- If something arrives outside a round — a text, a forwarded email — I'll hold
  it politely until the round opens, so it doesn't get lost or done twice.

**Why a limit?** Because it's what lets me quote a fixed price and hold a
launch date. Almost every project fits comfortably in two rounds. If you want
a third, it's **$[X]** and I'll happily do it — just ask.

**What isn't feedback:** anything we didn't agree in the requirements
document. That's not a problem — I'll quote it as a small separate piece so it
never quietly delays the launch. Good ideas are welcome; they just need a
price.
