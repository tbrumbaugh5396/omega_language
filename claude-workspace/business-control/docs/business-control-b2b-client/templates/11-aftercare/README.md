# Stage 10 — Keeping it, and getting paid for it
| Document | Goes to | When |
|---|---|---|
| `care-plan-agreement.md` | **Client** | Offered at handover, while goodwill is highest |
| `support-and-defects.md` | **Client** | With the plan — what is a bug and what is a change |
| `monitoring-and-incidents.md` | **Client** | With the plan — what is watched, who gets called |
| `security-and-compliance.md` | **Client** | With the plan — patching, access, the compliance scope |
| `growth-retainer.md` | **Client** | First review after launch, never at handover |
| `testimonial-request.md` | **Client** | 3–4 weeks after launch, once results exist |

Sell the care plan at handover. Two months later they've forgotten how much
they needed you, and a cold pitch for a maintenance contract is a much harder
conversation than a warm one on the day you handed over something that works.

Ask for the testimonial once there are numbers to quote. "It looks great" is
worth less than "enquiries up 40% in two months".

## The plan is four documents, not one

The care plan is the price and the promise. The three schedules are what the
promise means, and they are where every later argument is settled: whether a
report is a bug (support), whether an outage should have been caught
(monitoring), whether a patch was owed and when (security).

Send all four together. A client who signs a one-page plan and later reads
"uptime monitoring ✓" as "you will never be down" is a client you gave that
reading to.

**The growth retainer is not aftercare.** It is sold separately, priced
separately and cancelled separately — the care plan keeps the site working,
which they should never be able to lose by pausing an ad campaign.

**Gate:** care plan signed or declined in writing. Where it is signed, the
three schedules are signed with it — [ongoing support agreed] in the ERP is
that, not a verbal yes.

**Running it** is [running a care plan](../../procedures/running-a-care-plan.md).
Selling a cadence you don't then keep is worse than not selling it.
