## What "content changes" covers

Text edits, image swaps, adding blog posts or team members, price updates,
small layout tweaks within the existing design.

**Not covered:** new page templates, new functionality, redesigns, new
integrations. Those are quoted separately — plan members get the discount
above.

**Hours don't roll over.** They reserve capacity in the schedule, which is the
thing you're actually buying. Unused hours in a quiet month are what makes
same-day response possible in a busy one.

**Overflow** is billed at $[X]/hour, or buy an update block at $[X] for [10]
hours.

## What isn't included

- Content writing or photography
- SEO campaigns or advertising
- Fixing problems caused by Client edits _(fixed, then billed hourly)_
- Third-party service costs — domain, premium plugins, stock, email
- Recovery from a compromise caused by credentials shared outside the plan
- Migration to another host or platform

## How to pitch it — internal note

Don't lead with features. Lead with the thing they're actually worried about:

> "The site's live and it's yours. The only thing worth thinking about is who
> keeps it updated — software patches, backups, and the small edits that pile
> up. You can absolutely do it yourself, and if you'd rather not think about
> it, the plan is $[X] a month and includes [N] hours of changes so you're not
> paying me hourly for a typo."

Then stop talking.

Roughly a third say yes at handover. Of those who don't, a good number come
back within six months — usually the first time something breaks. Follow up at
three months with something useful (a performance note, a security advisory),
not a sales email.
