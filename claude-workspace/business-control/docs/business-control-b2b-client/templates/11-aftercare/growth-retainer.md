## Who owns what

**You own every account** — ads, analytics, search console, business profile —
and the Studio works inside them with named access. When this ends, we come
out and everything stays where it is. Retainers where the agency holds the
accounts are how clients end up hostage; that isn't sold here.

**Work product** — briefs, copy, landing pages, campaign structures — is yours
on payment, per clause [8] of the agreement.

- A named person who can approve copy within **[3] working days**

  that were worth it, jobs that weren't

The single most common reason a growth retainer underperforms is approvals
sitting for three weeks. Nothing on this page fixes that.

## Reporting, and what is honest to claim

Each report says: what was done, what moved, what it cost, and what happens
next. In that order, in plain numbers — sessions, enquiries, cost per enquiry,
revenue where it can be tracked. Rankings are context, not the result.

**What the Studio will not do:** report on a metric because it looks good,
claim a rise that came from a season or a price change, or promise a position.
Anyone guaranteeing a #1 ranking is either buying ads or lying.

**Expect [3–6] months** before organic work compounds. Paid can move in a
week. Both are said up front so month two is not a crisis.

## How to pitch it — internal note

Not at handover. The care plan is the handover conversation, and stacking two
retainers on one day gets you neither.

Raise this at the **first review after launch**, with the baseline in hand:
"You're getting [N] enquiries a month from [X] visits. Here's where the other
[Y] visits are going." A number they can check beats a package they have to
believe.

Sell Foundation to a client who isn't sure. Momentum and Campaign are for
clients whose numbers already prove a visit is worth something — if they can't
tell you what an enquiry is worth, sell them the baseline first.
