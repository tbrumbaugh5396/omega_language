motion · the spot & the page

A playable 14-second film and a scroll-driven landing page, both built from the same six beats. Everything here is live SVG and CSS — nothing is a video file, so it stays sharp at any size, weighs a few kilobytes, and can be screen-recorded straight into a real cut.

Why it works as engineering, not just as art

Nothing but transform & opacityEvery animation is compositor-only — no layout, no paint. It holds 60fps on a four-year-old phone, which is where most of your traffic is.
Under 20 KB, infinitely sharpThe whole film is vector and CSS. A video of this would be 8–15 MB, need three encodes, and still blur on a 5K display.
One asset, every ratiopreserveAspectRatio="slice" means the same file fills 16:9, 9:16 and 1:1. Cut the vertical for stories without re-mastering.
Reduced-motion honouredUnder prefers-reduced-motion the animation is skipped and the frame holds — the copy still reads, nobody gets sick.
The sea is proceduralTwo loops at 9s and 13s never re-sync, so the water never visibly repeats — the cheapest possible way to look expensive.
Screen-record itCapture the stage at 1600×900 and you have the actual spot. Use it as the animatic to sell the shoot, or ship it as the ad.

If you shoot it for real — the script

#ShotCameraOn screen
1Dark water, choppy, no horizon reference. Nothing human.Locked off, long lens, 120fps for slight slow-mo Days come in waves.
2A single amber droplet leaves the surface and rises.Macro, high-speed (1000fps), backlit—
3The droplet crosses the sun and holds — for one beat they are the same object.Long lens compression, practical sun or 18k through diffusion—
4Descent. Impact. A crown of spray, then nothing.High-speed macro on a black tank; single hard key — (the crack lands here)
5Rings spread. The chop flattens behind them into glass.Overhead, slow push out—
6The can, standing in still water, condensation forming. Bubbles rise inside on a cutaway.Turntable macro, warm key, cool fillZen comes in a can.
The one shot to spend the budget on: #3, the eclipse. It's the only frame that can't be faked cheaply and the only one people will remember. Shoot #2 and #4 in the same high-speed session on a black tank — they're the same setup — and get #1 and #5 from a drone or a tank overhead plate. #6 is a tabletop day. Two shoot days total, and the animatic above tells the crew exactly what the cut needs.
