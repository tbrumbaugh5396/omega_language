AV Trainer — start here

1) Double-click:  command_utilities/Install AV Trainer.command
   (one-time setup — builds a private .venv inside this folder)

2) Double-click:  command_utilities/Start AV Trainer.command
   Your browser opens at http://127.0.0.1:8910

3) On first launch you'll see a landing page — click "Sign up" to create
   your account. Each account keeps its own private data on this machine.
   Your profile (top-right) lets you rename yourself, change your password,
   log out, back up, restore, or load starter content.

Stop the server:  command_utilities/Stop AV Trainer.command
Desktop app icon: command_utilities/Install App Icon.command
Uninstall:        command_utilities/Uninstall AV Trainer.command
                  (keeps your data unless you say otherwise)

Use headphones. Half the drills are ear drills, and laptop speakers cannot
reproduce anything below about 150 Hz — which is exactly where several of
them live.

Want example content first?
  Profile menu → "Load starter content", or double-click
  command_utilities/Seed Starter Content.command
  You get three pieces at different stages with real postmortems, a week of
  practice, and the theory items those breaks pulled in. It is there to show
  you the shape of the loop, not to pretend you have a history.


WHAT THIS IS

A training environment built on one claim: intuition is amortized search
over trained perceptual features. Expensive deliberate work gets distilled
by volume into a fast policy that runs feedforward. That claim dictates the
build order, and the app is arranged around it:

  Perception first     — taste and discrimination are trainable
  Volume second        — the policy is found by search under feedback
  Articulation apart   — explaining is its own network, trained separately
  Theory pulled        — read what answers the problem you actually hit

Three tracks run in parallel, not in sequence: MAKE (one finished piece a
week), TOOLS (one tool until it is invisible), THEORY (raided, not marched
through). Perceptual training runs inside all three.


SUGGESTED FIRST SESSION

  · Today — accept this week's brief. It arrives with a constraint; keep it.
    Write the rubric before you start making anything.
  · Train → Drills — ten minutes. Pick one ear drill and one eye drill.
    Every answer comes back with the mechanism, which is where the
    vocabulary gets built.
  · Lab — open "SDF shapes" and drag the blend knob until the join reads as
    cast rather than cut. Then break it on purpose.
  · Analyze — drop in one reference you love. Describe before you judge.
  · Library — do not read it front to back. Search a symptom instead:
    "halo", "buried vocal", "reads as mechanical".


THE ONE THING THAT MATTERS

Ship the piece, then write the postmortem honestly: what reads wrong, the
mechanism you think caused it, and what you will read to fix it. The app
uses that to pull the theory in with your failure attached to it. Everything
else here is support for that loop.
